Section 1 — Business architecture, unit economics, moat & visibility

Model: a single-commodity gold producer with a hub-and-spoke model in WA. The Mt Magnet hub (resources 10 Moz) is fed by its own pits and underground mines plus trucked high-grade ore from Never Never/Dalgaranga, which came with the Spartan takeover (Jul-2025, about A$2.4B in scrip and cash). Rebecca-Roe (Goldfields) is the second development leg (140–150 koz in FY30). Edna May was sold (Jul-2026).

Moat classification: cost advantage (high-grade underground ore) plus efficient scale (owned processing infrastructure in a district that juniors cannot easily replicate). Competitive insulation is 3/5. Moat score 55/100.

Durability: static to widening through FY30, as the reserve grew 79% and discovery cost is only about A$55/oz. The eroding force is reserve depletion. On reserves alone, Never Never grade declines after the early 2030s; the Mar-2025 plan put the Mt Magnet output peak in FY33–35. WA labour and energy cost inflation is the nearer-term erosion: AISC has risen from A$1,973 (Q4 FY26) to A$2,150–2,350 (FY27 guide).

Build-vs-buy / copy risk: a well-capitalised incumbent cannot copy an orebody. The only way in is to buy it. No platform or hyperscaler risk applies, and customers (refiners or bullion banks) have no in-house alternative. M&A optionality: Ramelius is a credible target for Northern Star, Evolution, Gold Fields or Newmont at a typical 25–40% premium ASSUMED. That supports a soft floor, but a deal is not modelled.

Customer ROI: gold is a perfectly liquid, price-taking product. There is no pricing power, and no budget-freeze risk on volumes. Customer-ROI defensibility is 6/10: the product is always sellable, but the price is exogenous.
