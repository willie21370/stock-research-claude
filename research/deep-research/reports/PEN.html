Why this advice: In 2026 Panoro became a much bigger company in one step. It took Block G in Equatorial Guinea from 14.25% to 54.625% (bought from Kosmos) and bought 9.09% of the CI-27 gas field in Côte d'Ivoire from DNO. Pro forma production rose to ~20,800 boe/d, against 10,263 bopd in 2025. 2P reserves are now about 95 MMboe. At today's price the stock trades at ~2.5x 2027E EV/EBITDA and ~5.5x 2027E P/E, with a ~5% cash distribution yield. The cost of that growth: 24% more shares in 2026, a USD 300M 10.25% bullet bond due Dec-2029, and a H1-26 net loss of USD 59.4M (mostly non-cash hedge marks and an impairment). Our reverse DCF says NOK 27.55 already discounts about USD 66–67/bbl Brent held flat, against a base deck of USD 76 in 2027 falling to USD 70. That leaves a probability-weighted 36-month price of NOK 29.73. That is +7.9% in price, or about +24% including distributions, far below the 100% hurdle. Existing holders are paid to wait. New capital should wait for NOK 20–23 or for proof that MaBoMo Phase 2 is on stream.

Section 1 — Business architecture, unit economics, moat & visibility

Business model. Panoro is a non-operating (mostly) upstream E&P with four producing positions:

  • Equatorial Guinea: Block G at 54.625% (Ceiba and Okume, operated by Trident). Q2-26 pro forma output 9,456 bopd net.
  • Gabon: Dussafu Marin, operated by BW Energy (17.5% WI ASSUMED). 4,599 bopd.
  • Tunisia: TPS fields. 1,474 bopd.
  • Côte d'Ivoire: 9.09% of CI-27 gas from mid-Sep-2026. ~3,300 boe/d.

Revenue is cargo liftings priced against Brent, plus gas sold to the Ivorian state.

Moat classification: none in the classic sense. Competitive insulation 2/5. The only durable edges are (i) cost position: Dussafu is a low-cost shallow-water development with a PSC extended to 2053, and (ii) deal-sourcing: buying non-core barrels from exiting players (Kosmos, DNO, earlier Tullow) at USD 3–9/boe of 2P. Durability: static to eroding. Natural decline at Block G (no drilling until 2028) is the force eroding the base, and it shows up from 2029.

Build-vs-buy / copy risk:

  • Any capitalised E&P can buy similar assets. The barrier is regional relationships and appetite for African PSC risk, not capital.
  • "Hyperscaler" replication does not apply.
  • The customers are commodity traders. They cannot "build in-house", and they do not need to.
  • M&A optionality: Panoro is a credible target at ~USD 650M EV. Plausible buyers are DNO (already 5%), BW Energy (Dussafu operator) or a regional consolidator. A typical E&P premium of 25–40% would give NOK 34–39. This offers a soft valuation floor, not a catalyst we can date.

Customer ROI / loss avoidance: not applicable in the usual sense. Panoro sells a fungible commodity at index prices and has no pricing power. In a "budget freeze" (demand shock) it takes the full price hit. Customer-ROI defensibility: 3/10.
