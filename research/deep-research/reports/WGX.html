Section 1 — Business architecture, unit economics, moat & contractual visibility

Business model. Westgold is a Western Australian gold producer with two regions. Murchison covers the Meekatharra hub (Bluebird–South Junction, Paddy's Flat) and the Cue hub (Big Bell, Great Fingall, Crown Prince open pit), with 8.1 Moz of resources. Southern Goldfields came with the 2024 Karora merger: Beta Hunt underground, the Higginsville and Lakewood mills, and 6.2 Moz of resources. All gold is sold unhedged at spot.

Moat classification: mild cost advantage / efficient scale from owned regional mills and a consolidated tenement position. Competitive insulation: 2/5. The moat is static. Reserve growth (+41% in FY26 at A$27/oz) shows the land package works. The force most likely to erode it is WA labour and contractor cost inflation, which is already visible in the 12% rise in FY27 AISC guidance and could compress margins further through FY28.

Build-vs-buy / copy risk

  • Could an incumbent replicate it? Building four processing hubs and a 14 Moz resource base in the Murchison and Kambalda districts would take about 7–10 years and more than A$2B. Replication is not realistic. Acquisition is the route.
  • Platform / customer build risk: Not applicable. Gold is a fungible commodity sold to refiners.
  • Is Westgold an M&A target? Plausibly yes. The Cue hub sits close to Ramelius's Mt Magnet operations, and the Southern Goldfields assets would fit a Kalgoorlie-district consolidator. Plausible acquirers include Northern Star, Ramelius, Genesis and international mid-tiers seeking WA exposure. A typical premium of 25–40% gives about A$7.75–8.70/share. We put the probability of a bid within 36 months at about 10% ASSUMED. That gives a soft floor in the conservative case, not a base-case driver.

Customer ROI / loss-avoidance

Westgold has no customer-ROI franchise. Its product is a monetary metal sold at the LBMA-linked spot price. Pricing power is zero. The flip side is that demand never needs to be won: every ounce sells immediately. Customer-ROI defensibility: 3/10 (perfect liquidity, but no price premium).

Historically, only a minority (we estimate about 30–40%) of ASX mid-tier gold producers deliver a three-year growth outlook on schedule. Slippage of one year or more is the modal outcome, and Westgold has already slipped once. The base case is therefore not top-decile on growth, but it does need above-median execution. That is why base-case likelihood is 50/100 and not higher.
