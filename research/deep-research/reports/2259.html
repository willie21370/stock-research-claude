Executive summary

  1. A great operator at a full price. H1 2026 revenue doubled to US$3.987bn, attributable profit rose 179% to US$1.451bn, output rose 44% to 27.3t and operating cash flow quadrupled to US$1.80bn. The quality is not in doubt. At HK$203.6 the stock trades at 22–23x house EPS, more than double the 9.2x peer median. It is also above every dated sell-side target (HK$150–189).
  2. Earnings are a leveraged gold call, and the house deck is flat to down. A ±10% move in gold changes 2027E profit by ±18%. With gold at US$4,150 easing to US$3,900 by 2029, rising volume (58.5t to 70t) only offsets the lower price. Base EPS stays around HK$8.8 through 2031. Screening consensus (HK$12.88 for 2027E, 10 analysts) looks stale, and is 35% above house 2028E.
  3. The M&A growth engine hit a wall. The C$5.5bn all-cash Allied Gold takeover (Sadiola, Kurmuk, CDI) was terminated on 29 Jul 2026 because the conditions could not be met (“broader external factors” for cross-border deals). It became a US$295m 9.2% stake. Acquisitions are the Zijin group's core skill, and this one showed that Western and host-state resistance limits it.
  4. A fortress balance sheet. Net cash was US$3.44bn at 30 Jun 2026 against borrowings of only US$430m. FCF of about US$3bn a year funds growth capex and a 35% payout (first interim dividend of HK$1.50). There is no liquidity, covenant or dilution risk. The stress case is purely about price and multiple.
  5. The reverse DCF is demanding. At HK$203.6 and a 12% WACC, the market is pricing flat gold of about US$6,300/oz from 2027 (US$5,000 at a 9% WACC) with production rising to 73t. That is 20–50% above spot. The bull case is already in the price.
  6. Ownership is a headwind, not a floor. The parent owns about 85% and the float is about 15%. The parent's 12-month post-IPO lock-up ended around 30 Sep 2026, so a placement to widen the float is a live overhang (30% probability in 12 months, ASSUMED). A buyback is near-impossible, and a parent privatisation is the only M&A floor.

Verdict: SELL (new capital: Avoid; existing holders: Sell or trim to ≤1.2%). Base-case targets: 12 months HK$149.9, 24 months HK$148.9, 36 months HK$149.1. Probability-weighted 36-month target: HK$156.5 (−23.1%).

Meets the return hurdle? No. Probability of reaching 2× (HK$407) within 36 months is about 1%.

Best in category? No. For new capital, K92 Mining (PW +32%, about 7x 2027E P/E) and Aura Minerals (PW +23%, buyback plus a growth pipeline) offer positive expected returns at a third of the multiple. Existing holders get parent-backed diversification and a dividend, but at this price the expected return is negative, so trimming is preferred.

Single greatest upside driver: gold back above US$5,000 and holding there (bull EPS HK$14–15 by 2029–30). Most likely thesis-killer (for longs): gold drifts to US$3,600–3,900 while the 22x premium multiple compresses toward 15x.

It listed in Hong Kong on 30 Sep 2025 at HK$71.59, raising about US$3.2bn (the world's second-largest IPO of 2025), and closed day one at HK$120.60 VERIFIED. Customer ROI / loss-avoidance

The valuation does not depend on cheap capital: the company is net cash. The two fail on different timelines, and the flow risk is the nearer one.
