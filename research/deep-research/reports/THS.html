Section 1 — Business architecture, unit economics, moat & contractual visibility

Business model & moat classification

Tharisa plc (Cyprus-incorporated; LSE main market and JSE primary) owns 74% of Tharisa Minerals ASSUMED. That company operates the Tharisa open-pit mine on the western limb of the Bushveld (UG2/MG chromitite reefs). It produces two co-products:

  • PGM concentrate: 138.3 koz 6E in FY25; FY26 guidance 145–165 koz.
  • Chrome concentrate: 1.56 Mt in FY25; FY26 guidance 1.50–1.65 Mt. Chrome is sold and shipped through the in-house Arxo marketing and logistics arm.

Growth comes from two projects:

  • The Apollo/Orion underground transition: $547m over 10 years; first blast 31 Mar 2026; steady state of 255 kt/month by Q3 FY29.
  • Karo Platinum on Zimbabwe's Great Dyke: effective interest 78.81% × 85% = 67.0%; $546m capex; first ore Q4 CY2027.

Moat classification: cost advantage (co-product credits) only. Competitive insulation: 2/5.

Moat durability: static to slightly eroding through FY29. Shallow, cheap open-pit ore is being replaced by higher-cost underground bord-and-pillar ore during the transition. The force most likely to erode the moat is rand cost inflation (labour, Eskom power, royalties) during FY27–29. Karo then partly restores scale from FY29.

Build-vs-buy / copy risk

  • Incumbent replication: the orebody cannot be copied. A major (Valterra, Implats/Zimplats, Northam, Sibanye) could build a comparable Great Dyke open pit for ~$0.5–0.6bn over 4–5 years. Zimplats and Unki already operate there.
  • Hyperscaler / platform: not applicable.
  • Customer in-house build: Chinese stainless and ferrochrome mills can (and do) buy SA chrome from many producers. Refiners integrate upstream only by acquisition.
  • M&A optionality: credible but family-gated. The obvious acquirer is Valterra: it is Karo's offtaker and runs the neighbouring Unki mine on the Great Dyke. Implats/Zimplats and Chinese chrome consumers are other possibilities. A typical premium is 30–40%. The Pouroulis family stake (~35–40% ASSUMED) gives control. We put the probability of a bid at ~5% a year. This is a soft floor, not a valuation pillar.

Customer ROI / loss-avoidance

Tharisa's products prevent no specific customer loss. They are price-taking commodity inputs: autocatalysts, jewellery, investment and hydrogen-related demand for PGMs, and stainless steel for chrome. Value is set by the Pt/Pd/Rh benchmarks and CIF China chrome indices. There is no price premium, and pricing power in a budget freeze is zero. Customer-ROI defensibility: 3/10.
