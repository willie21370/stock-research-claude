Section 1 — Business architecture, unit economics, moat & visibility

Business model. GeoPark is a Latin American upstream oil producer. It sold its Ecuador and Brazil assets in 2025, so it now operates in two countries. In Colombia (2026 guide 24.5–26.0k boe/d) the core is the operated Llanos 34 block. It also holds the non-operated CPO-5 block and growth blocks Llanos 123/87; Llanos 123 is being drilled in 2026 VERIFIED. Block working interests (Llanos 34 ~45% operated, with Parex as partner) are background knowledge ASSUMED. In Argentina (2026 guide 2.5–4.0k boe/d) the assets are Loma Jarillosa Este and Puesto Silva Oeste: 100% operated, with 5% of PSO transferred to provincial GyP, in Vaca Muerta's black-oil window. Both were acquired from Pluspetrol for $115M and closed 16 Oct 2025 VERIFIED. Revenue is crude sold at Brent-linked prices.

Moat classification: cost advantage and operator know-how on mature, low-decline conventional fields. Competitive insulation is 2/5. GeoPark is a price-taker, and its Vaca Muerta acreage is smaller and less proven than YPF's or Vista's.

Moat durability: eroding in Colombia, where Llanos 34 is mature and the state is hostile to new exploration contracts. It is being rebuilt in Argentina. The main erosion force is natural decline at Llanos 34 combined with a lack of new Colombian acreage (2026–2030). In Argentina, competition for rigs, frac crews and evacuation capacity during the basin boom (2026–28) is the pressure point.

Build-vs-buy / copy risk: replication is not the issue for a commodity producer, and hyperscaler or customer in-house risk does not apply. M&A optionality is unusually concrete here:

  • Parex bid $9.00 (Sep 2025), which is about $8.85 on today's share count.
  • Parex is GeoPark's Llanos 34 partner (assumed) and has just consolidated Frontera's Colombian assets.
  • Gilinski holds ≥20%.

A sale of Colombia to Parex, or a take-out at a 25–40% premium, is plausible. We put its probability at about 10–15% within 36 months. We do not add it to the base case; it sits inside the Aggressive scenario.

Customer ROI / loss avoidance: not applicable in the usual sense, because crude is fungible. Customer-ROI defensibility is 2/10 and pricing power is nil.

Section 2 — Multi-year financial forecast & consensus model

Method.

  • Revenue = production × 365 × 0.86 (sales-to-production, calibrated to Q2) × (Brent − $9 differential).
  • EBITDA = revenue − production × cash cost/boe: $24 in 2027, $22 in 2028, $21.5 from 2029.
  • D&A is $13/$14/$14.5 per boe. Net interest = 8.5% × net debt + ~$14M.
  • Tax rate is 50% at Brent ≥$80, 44% at $70–79, 40% at $62–69 and 36% below that. This reflects Colombia's Brent-linked surtax on top of the 35% statutory rate.
  • 2026 = H1 actual (revenue $271.7M, EBITDA $144.4M) plus a modelled H2 held down by hedge ceilings.
  • Anchored years: 2026 (guidance and actuals) and the 2028 volume/EBITDA guideline. Extrapolation starts in 2027, where no guidance has been published yet (due early Dec 2026). The oil deck is a house deck in every year. ASSUMED
