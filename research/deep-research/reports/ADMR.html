Executive summary

  1. Coal funds everything, and it is very profitable. 1H26 revenue was US$583.9m (+31.5%) and attributable profit was US$174.2m (+24%), with no aluminium revenue yet. Coal sold at US$185.9/t in 2Q26 (+24% YoY) at a 52% EBITDA margin. For every US$10/t the coal price moves, net profit moves about US$48m (about Rp19 EPS).
  2. The smelter is producing, but not yet earning. KAI (the smelter subsidiary, PT Kalimantan Aluminium Industry) was commissioned in Dec 2025 and shipped 35,063 t in June 2026. It lost US$45.5m in 1H26 and was holding about US$319m of aluminium inventory, which is why 1H26 operating cash flow was −US$173m. The full 500ktpa rate is now targeted for end-2026, versus "early 2026" before. We model 260kt sold in 2026 and 480kt in 2027.
  3. The balance sheet has swung from net cash to leveraged. Bank debt rose to US$1,158m and a US$690m lease of a power plant from a sister company (AADI) was added. Gross debt is about US$1.9bn, roughly 1.9x our 2027E EBITDA. That is manageable while coal is above US$150/t, but the power lease is a related-party arrangement that sets the smelter's biggest cost.
  4. We are well below the Street, on purpose. We forecast EPS of Rp133 / 175 / 195 for 2026–28, versus the only published broker model at Rp180 / 227 / 283. The gap comes from three things: our assumed 25% minority in KAI, the lease costs, and a coal price of US$175/t. At our numbers the stock trades at 8.1x 2027E, so it is fair to modestly cheap, not deeply discounted. The 16-analyst mean target of Rp2,373 assumes the broker earnings path.
  5. The skew does not meet the mandate. The probability-weighted 36-month target is Rp1,904 (+34%) and the stress case is Rp660 (−54%). That is an upside/downside ratio of 0.64:1 against a required 3:1. The risk-budget cap is a maximum position of 1.87%.

Section 1 — Business architecture, unit economics, moat & contractual visibility

Business. ADMR has two businesses. (1) Metallurgical coal from five Coal Contract of Work concessions in East and Central Kalimantan (the Maruwai/AMC complex), producing hard and semi-soft coking coal with low ash and low sulphur. It produced 7.41Mt and sold 6.28Mt in FY25. (2) Primary aluminium from PT Kalimantan Aluminium Industry (KAI) at the North Kalimantan green industrial park (KIPI). Phase 1 is 500ktpa, powered by a coal-fired plant leased from a sister company. The plan is 1.5Mtpa across three phases, with later phases intended to use hydro power.

Moat classification: cost advantage plus scarce resource. Competitive insulation grade 2/5. ADMR is a price-taker in both commodities. Its edge is resource quality (a large, high-quality coking-coal deposit close to Asian steel mills) and integration with group power and logistics. Moat durability: static to slightly eroding. The main eroding force is decarbonisation of steel (electric-arc furnaces, DRI) after about 2030 in China, Japan and Korea, which lowers the coking-coal intensity of steel. The second is carbon border taxes on coal-powered aluminium (EU CBAM definitive phase from 2026), which push KAI's metal toward discounted, non-premium markets unless hydro power arrives.

Build-vs-buy / copy risk. An incumbent cannot easily copy the coal side: a new Kalimantan coking-coal mine needs a concession, infrastructure and 5–8 years. A 500ktpa aluminium smelter can be replicated for roughly US$1.5–2.5bn in 3–4 years, and Chinese-backed Indonesian smelters are being built. A hyperscaler or platform "feature" threat does not apply to a miner. Customers (steel mills) could build mines in-house, as some Japanese and Indian mills hold equity stakes, but only at high cost. As an M&A target, ADMR is controlled by about 69% by its parent, so an outside takeover is unlikely. A buy-out of minorities by the parent at a 20–30% premium is a low-probability floor (about 5%).

Customer ROI / loss avoidance. Low-ash coking coal reduces coke-oven ash and raises blast-furnace productivity, so steelmakers pay a quality premium that they measure. Aluminium is a fungible commodity (LME plus regional premium). Pricing power in a downturn is nil: prices follow benchmarks. Customer-ROI defensibility: 4/10.
