Executive summary

  1. Operations are at a record, but the 2026 hedges captured little of the war windfall. Q2-26 adj. EBITDA was $415M (+75% y/y), production 107.5 kboe/d, and net income $172M ($3.2/ADR).   2. Rincón de Aranda is the volume engine. Oil rises from 23.4 kbpd (Q2-26) to a 28 kbpd exit in 2026 and a 45 kbpd plateau in 2027, roughly doubling oil volume. 2026 capex is ~$1bn and falls to ~$700M in 2027 excluding urea, so core FCF turns strongly positive from 2027.
  3. The urea plant is the swing factor. The $2.7bn, 2.1 Mt/yr Bahía Blanca plant (FID 17 Jul 2026, RIGI-approved, Tecnimont EPC ~€1.3bn) absorbs roughly $2.5bn of capex in 2027–29. Management guides net leverage to peak at ~2x. The plant adds ~$400M of EBITDA from 2031 if it is built on time and on budget, and nothing before 2030.
  4. Cheap, but the cheapness is earned. At 6.6x 2027E EPS and ~3x EV/EBITDA, the market discounts Argentine sovereign risk and four years of negative FCF. DCF fair value is $85 (+11%), and the probability-weighted 36m target is $88.7 (+16%).
  5. Street targets look stretched. The mean target is $121 (11 analysts, 9 Buy). Reaching it needs ~10x 2027E EPS or a Brent deck above $80. EPS consensus rests on only 3 analysts, and our 2028E EPS is 17% below it.

Section 1 — Business architecture, unit economics, moat & visibility

Business model. Pampa operates four segments:

  1. Oil & gas: Vaca Muerta shale gas, plus fast-growing shale oil at Rincón de Aranda.
  2. Power generation: the largest independent generator in Argentina, with a thermal and wind fleet of ~5.5 GW ASSUMED.
  3. Petrochemicals: styrene, polymers and reforming.
  4. Holding and others: co-control stakes in TGS (gas transport and processing) and Transener (high-voltage transmission).

A fifth segment, fertilisers (Fértil Pampa urea), is under construction for YE2029.

Q2-26 adj. EBITDA mix: O&G $182M (44%), power $155M (37%), petchem $20M (5%), holding/affiliates ~$58M (14%) VERIFIED (holding share DERIVED as a residual).

Moat. Pampa's moat combines a cost advantage from vertical integration (own gas into own efficient CCGTs, and later into urea, where gas and power are ~70% of cost) with efficient scale in regulated network assets. Competitive insulation is graded 3/5. The moat is static to slightly widening: it widens if urea is executed, because Pampa can then monetise its own gas at export-parity urea prices. The force most likely to erode it is power-market deregulation. The WEM reform opens the spot market and contracts to competition, and new entrants (renewables plus batteries) could compress thermal spreads from ~2028.

Build-vs-buy / copy risk:

  • A capitalised incumbent such as YPF or Pan American could replicate gas-to-power, but it would need ~$3–5bn and 4–5 years to build an equivalent thermal fleet. There is no hyperscaler-style platform risk.
  • Customers (CAMMESA, distributors, industrials) cannot self-supply at scale.
  • M&A optionality: Pampa is family-controlled, so a takeover is unlikely. A partial sale of the TGS or Transener stakes is plausible and would crystallise value at an assumed 20–30% premium to look-through value. That puts a soft floor under the valuation.

Customer ROI / loss-avoidance: Pampa supplies firm capacity that keeps the grid stable through winter peaks, and domestic gas that replaces imported LNG at roughly 2–3x the cost. The value is real and measured: Argentina's import bill falls. Pricing, however, is set by policy (CAMMESA tariffs, Plan Gas), not by Pampa. Customer-ROI defensibility: 5/10.
