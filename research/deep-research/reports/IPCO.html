Section 1 — Business architecture, unit economics, moat & visibility

Business model. IPC is an upstream producer in three countries.

  • Canada (~90% of volumes): Suffield/Brooks gas and conventional oil, the Onion Lake thermal project, and the new Blackrod SAGD project.
  • Malaysia: the Bertam field.
  • France: the Paris and Aquitaine basins.

Asset composition comes from background knowledge ASSUMED; the country list is VERIFIED. The Q2-26 production mix was 53% heavy crude, 13% light/medium crude and 34% natural gas VERIFIED. Revenue comes from commodity sales at market prices.

Moat classification: efficient scale and cost position on a long-life resource. The 2P RLI is 31 years, reserve replacement was 277% in 2025, and contingent resources are 1,224 MMboe VERIFIED. Competitive insulation: 2/5. IPC is a pure price-taker on WCS, Brent and AECO.

Moat durability: static to slightly widening through 2027. Blackrod converts contingent resources into producing reserves, and SAGD declines are low once plateau is reached. The force most likely to erode it is the post-payout Alberta bitumen royalty step-up around 2030–31, together with any federal emissions-cap or carbon-cost escalation. A narrowing in WCS egress is the market risk (2027–29).

Build-vs-buy / copy risk: replication is not the relevant question for a commodity producer. A competitor would need ~5 years and ~US$1bn (ASSUMED) to permit and build an equivalent SAGD phase. Platform or customer in-house risk does not apply. M&A optionality: IPC is a credible target for a Canadian oil-sands consolidator. Long-life SAGD barrels have changed hands at a premium; Cenovus/MEG in 2025 is an example (ASSUMED). The ~43% Lundin stake means any deal needs the family's consent. We put the chance of a bid within 36 months at ~8% and include it only inside the Aggressive scenario.

Customer ROI / loss avoidance: not applicable; crude and gas are fungible. Customer-ROI defensibility: 2/10. Pricing power is nil.

Section 2 — Multi-year financial forecast & consensus model

Method (house model, USD). Production is split into legacy assets (41k boe/d in 2026, 65% liquids, declining 3%/yr in base) and Blackrod (100% bitumen). Pricing and costs:

  • Legacy liquids realize Brent − $12 and net 85% after royalties; gas earns US$14/boe and nets 95%.
  • Blackrod bitumen realizes Brent − $4 (WTI) − $13 (WCS) − $5 (bitumen/diluent). The pre-payout royalty is 5%, stepping toward ~15% in 2031 as payout approaches.
  • Opex is US$20/boe for legacy and US$11/bbl for Blackrod at plateau, with 2.5% inflation. G&A is US$25M.
  • 2026 includes an US$85M drag (hedges and start-up) so that OCF reconciles to guidance at ~US$310M. From 2027 a US$1.5/boe leakage is applied.
  • DD&A is US$15/boe for legacy and US$9/bbl for Blackrod. Book tax is 22% and cash tax 3–7% of revenue (Canadian tax pools).
  • Interest: 7.5% bond plus bank debt.

Anchored years: 2026 (guidance plus Q2 actuals); 2027–28 volumes rest on Blackrod plateau guidance. Extrapolation starts in 2027 for prices and costs; no 2027 guidance has been issued yet (due ~Feb 2027). FY2025 revenue base: ~US$700M ASSUMED. Revenue is net of royalties and excludes diluent pass-through. Street figures probably include diluent, which inflates their revenue line.

Bear world (Conservative): an Iran settlement reopens Hormuz and OPEC+ keeps unwinding cuts, so Brent sits at US$64–66 from 2027. WCS widens to US$14. Blackrod ramps a quarter late (21k average in 2027). Legacy margins compress to ~US$5–8/boe and buybacks slow to 40% of a thin FCF. Base world: the war premium fades, Brent averages US$75 in 2027 and US$70 after that. Blackrod hits 30k from 2028 and the NCIB runs at its cap. Bull world (Thesis): a lasting supply risk premium holds Brent at US$75–80. Blackrod reaches 31k with lower opex, and FCF exceeds US$400M in 2027–28.
