Section 1 — Business architecture, unit economics, moat & contractual visibility

Business model. Orezone is a two-asset gold producer. Bomboré (90% Orezone, 10% Burkina Faso state) is an open-pit operation about 85 km east of Ouagadougou, moving from oxide to hard-rock ore through a 2.5 Mtpa Stage 1 plant (commercial production 2026-01-16) and Stage 2A, which takes it to 5 Mtpa. Casa Berardi (100%) is an underground and open-pit mine with its own mill in the James Bay/Abitibi region of Quebec. It was acquired from Hecla on 2026-03-25 for $352M upfront and deferred, plus up to $241M contingent. Orezone is listed on the TSX and, since August 2025, on the ASX.

Moat classification: cost/asset advantage at the deposit level only. Competitive insulation is 2/5. Gold is a perfectly fungible price-taker product. The edge is a low strip ratio at shallow depth (<40 m average reserve pit depth), cheap brownfield expansion, and a second jurisdiction. Durability: static to eroding. Bomboré grades fall as oxide is replaced by hard rock, and the main eroding force is fiscal: Burkina Faso's rising royalty and state participation regime. That pressure is already present and could step up at any time in 2026–2028.

Build-vs-buy / copy risk. An incumbent cannot replicate a specific orebody, so copy risk is not the issue. A greenfield 300 koz/yr West African mine would cost $500M or more and take 4–6 years. There is no "hyperscaler" analogue, and customers (refiners and bullion banks) cannot build gold in-house. Orezone as a target: credible. Buyers could be West Africa-focused mid-tiers wanting Bomboré's scale, or Canadian producers wanting Abitibi infrastructure, either for Casa Berardi alone or for the whole company. A typical 25–40% control premium would imply C$4.00–4.45. The buyer pool for Burkina risk is narrow, so we treat M&A as a soft floor, not a valuation driver.

Customer ROI / loss-avoidance. Gold buyers are price-takers on both sides, so the product prevents no customer pain and has no pricing power beyond spot. Customer-ROI defensibility is 3/10. In a "budget freeze" (risk-on, falling gold) revenue moves one-for-one with spot.
