Section 1 — Business architecture, unit economics, moat & visibility

Business model & moat classification

GMS owns and charters self-propelled self-elevating support vessels (SESVs, or liftboats). These jack up beside offshore platforms and provide accommodation, crane capacity and deck space for maintenance, well intervention, EPC hook-up and offshore wind work. The fleet is 15 vessels after the Jan-26 purchase of a new mid-class unit VERIFIED. Large E-Class vessels generated $41.35M of H1-26 adj. revenue (49%) at a ~65% pre-D&A gross margin DERIVED. Customers are mainly Gulf NOCs and their EPC contractors. In 2026 the company added Europe, Latin America and Africa.

Moat type: efficient scale plus cost/supply advantage. The installed SESV fleet is ageing and the newbuild orderbook is near zero. Replacement cost sits well above the current fleet's book value, and NOC prequalification takes years. Competitive insulation: 3/5.

Durability: static to slightly widening through ~2028. Tight supply supports rate step-ups at each renewal. The force most likely to erode it is (1) a newbuild cycle if rates exceed ~$50k/day, which we put at 2029 or later given 2–3-year yard lead times, or (2) NOCs moving work to jack-up rigs, accommodation barges or Chinese-built units in a downturn.

Build-vs-buy / copy risk

  • Incumbent replication: a well-capitalised rival (ADES, Shelf-type drillers, regional OSV owners) could order units at ~$80–120M per E-Class equivalent ASSUMED, with 2–3 years to delivery. Building a 15-vessel fleet would take more than $1bn and 5+ years.
  • Platform/hyperscaler: not applicable.
  • Customer in-house: NOCs (ADNOC, Aramco) could own vessels, as their drilling arms do. What stops them is utilisation risk, crewing and capital priority. A real but low risk (~10% over 5 years).
  • M&A optionality: credible. The natural buyers are anchor holders (Mazrui, Seafox), regional consolidators (ADES, ADNOC Drilling/L&S-type groups) or private equity. A premium of 30–50% on a 5x P/E name is plausible, which puts a soft floor at ~1x book. ASSUMED

Customer ROI / loss-avoidance

Liftboats prevent production losses and safety failures. They let NOCs run maintenance and well work on ageing platforms without a drilling rig, at a fraction of rig day rates. The value is measurable to the customer (barrels kept online, campaign days saved). Pricing power rests on scarcity, not on any special feature. In a budget freeze, NOCs defer non-critical campaigns, but integrity maintenance is sticky. Customer-ROI defensibility: 6/10.
