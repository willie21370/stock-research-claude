Executive summary

  1. A record half that the market has already paid for. H1-26 revenue was US$914.2m (+58%), mine-operating earnings about US$620m (+124%) and attributable profit US$507m (+153%). Q2 alone earned US$275.8m on US$461.0m of revenue, a 71% mine-operating margin. The shares then rose about 75% from June to early September. At HK$227.80 they trade on 12.4x our FY26E EPS of HK$18.39 and 14.9x TTM, with spot metals near cycle highs.
  2. Jiama is a world-class orebody, and the growth plan is real but unpriced in detail. The 2026 NI 43-101 update lifted reserves to 665Mt (+51%) and measured resources to 623Mt (+523%). On 13 Aug management approved a plan to move from 50ktpd to about 160ktpd via a super pit. The PFS, capex, timetable and permits are all still open. We assume about US$3.2bn of capex and first ramp in 2031, so the payoff sits mostly beyond the 36-month window.
  3. Near-term volumes are falling, not rising. Gold output fell 18% in H1 (72.3koz), and the CSH open pit has been suspended since the 22 May slope failure. Copper is flat at about 66–68ktpa until expansion. Earnings growth from here therefore depends on metal prices. Our base deck assumes a modest fade (gold to US$3,800, copper to US$5.50/lb by 2029), which takes EPS to HK$12.4 in 2029E.
  4. The valuation does not clear the bar. The DCF (12% WACC) gives HK$170. The probability-weighted 36-month target is HK$179.6 (−21%), or −13% including dividends. The upside-to-stress ratio is 0.26:1, against a 3:1 requirement. The reverse DCF shows the price needs about US$1.7bn of steady-state post-expansion FCF, 41% above our base.
  5. Governance and single-jurisdiction risk cap the multiple. CNGG controls the company (about 40%), sales are believed to go to related parties, and both mines are in China, one of them in Tibet. Two geotechnical or safety stoppages in three years (Jiama 2023–24, CSH 2026) argue for a discount, not a premium.

Section 1 — Business architecture, unit economics, moat & contractual visibility

Business model & moat

The company has two operating mines. CSH (Inner Mongolia) is an open-pit, heap-leach gold mine, about 96.5% owned ASSUMED. It is late in life and an underground option is being studied. Jiama (Tibet, 100%) is a copper-gold-silver-molybdenum-lead-zinc polymetallic mine with 50ktpd design capacity. In FY2025, Jiama produced essentially all of the 70.9kt of copper and about 73koz of gold DERIVED; CSH supplied the rest of the 177koz gold total.

Moat classification: efficient scale plus a regulatory/intangible moat. Jiama’s 1,335Mt M&I resource is a tier-1 deposit, and its licences in Tibet would be close to impossible for a non-SOE to replicate. Competitive insulation is 3/5. A commodity price-taker has no pricing power, but its position on the cost curve and its licence are durable.

Moat durability: widening on resource (reserve +51% in 2026). Static to eroding on cost: the super-pit plan moves to a higher-tonnage, likely lower-grade operation, and Tibetan altitude, environmental scrutiny and labour costs rise. The force most likely to erode it is regulatory and environmental tightening on large open pits on the Tibetan plateau, which would bite at permitting in 2027–28.

Build-vs-buy / copy risk

  • Incumbent replication: not possible. Orebodies are not replicable, and the closest equivalents are Zijin’s Julong/Qulong and other Tibetan porphyries, which need 7–10 years and US$3–5bn each.
  • Hyperscaler or platform owner: not applicable.
  • Customer in-house: not applicable. The buyers are smelters and refiners, believed to include CNGG affiliates.
  • Acquisition target: CNGG could privatise the roughly 60% minority, or fold the company into its A-share sister Zhongjin Gold. Nothing has been announced. We put a 10% probability on such a deal within 36 months, at a typical 20–30% HK SOE privatisation premium. That is a soft floor only, because a premium to a lower future price does not protect today’s buyer.

Customer ROI / loss-avoidance

The company sells undifferentiated metal (gold doré, copper, moly and lead-zinc concentrates). There is no customer ROI case and no price premium; in a downturn the price realised is simply spot less treatment charges. Customer-ROI defensibility: 3/10.

3. Bias check
