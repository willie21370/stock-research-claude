Section 1 — Business architecture, unit economics, moat & contractual visibility

Business model & moat

Equinox mines and sells gold. After the Brazil sale (Jan 2026) and the Orla merger (31 Jul 2026), the portfolio is: Canada: Greenstone (Ontario, open pit), Musselwhite (Ontario, underground; from Orla) and Valentine (Newfoundland, open pit, first gold 2025); USA: Mesquite and Castle Mountain (California), plus South Railroad (Nevada, development); Mexico: Camino Rojo (heap leach) and Los Filos (suspended); Nicaragua: Libertad / Limon complex (from Calibre). VERIFIED on the asset list; the per-mine split is ASSUMED.

Moat classification: cost advantage none; efficient scale and intangibles (permits and reserves in Tier-1 jurisdictions) partial. Competitive insulation: 2/5. Gold is a perfectly fungible commodity, so the only durable edge is long-life reserves in safe jurisdictions, which are scarce, take 10+ years to permit and build, and are not replicable on demand.

Moat durability: static. Reserve replacement is the eroding force. Mesquite depletes before ~2031, and Camino Rojo oxides decline unless the underground is built. The pipeline (South Railroad, Castle Mountain Phase 2, Valentine Phase 2, Los Filos) offsets this only if it is permitted and executed by 2029–2030.

Build-vs-buy / copy risk

  • Incumbent replication: a new 1 Moz/yr Canadian/US portfolio would cost an estimated US$8–12bn of capex and 10–15 years of permitting. Replacement cost is the real barrier ASSUMED.
  • Platform/hyperscaler risk: not applicable.
  • Customer in-house build: not applicable. Buyers (refiners, bullion banks) take metal at spot.
  • M&A optionality: plausible acquirers are Agnico Eagle (Ontario adjacency), Newmont, Barrick and Kinross. A typical senior gold premium is 20–35%. The roughly $13bn size and the Nicaragua exposure shrink the buyer pool. Probability of a bid within 36 months is ~10% ASSUMED, so it is a soft floor rather than a thesis.

Customer ROI / loss avoidance

Gold buyers are not "customers" in the ROI sense. Demand comes from central banks, ETFs and jewellery, and prices are set at the LBMA. Pricing power is zero, and a customer budget freeze translates to the gold price, not to volume. Customer-ROI defensibility: 5/10: the product never goes unsold, but the company cannot set its price.

2. Self-audit for red flags

  • The screening file lists market cap at US$9.33bn, which uses pre-merger shares; the correct figure is ~US$12.9bn. Consensus forward P/Es of 9.2/5.9/7.6x are therefore not comparable with house multiples. Flagged, not corrected in the source.
  • 2026E EPS of $0.956 is on a weighted 891M shares (Orla for 5 months), while 2027E onward uses ~1,167M. This makes 2027E per-share growth negative (−4.5%) and drives the n.m. PEGs and the Rule-of-40 distortion. It is a share-base effect, not an operating decline.
  • The 2026E model uses a full-year $4,400 realised price and $1,700 cash cost. Q2 actuals ($4,256 / $1,816) were worse, so 2026E adjusted EPS ($0.96) needs a much stronger H2: ≈$0.50/sh on ~1,150M shares, assuming Q1-26 adjusted net income of ~$150M. That is plausible given Orla’s lower-cost ounces and the guidance midpoint, but unproven.
  • The stress case uses 25–40x P/E on near-zero EPS. The multiples are high by construction, so values are cross-checked against EV/EBITDA (~5x). The P/E column alone would look internally odd.
  • The DCF terminal value (~72% of EV) assumes reserve replacement. This flatters the DCF relative to a life-of-mine NAV, which we could not build.
  • Base 36-mo price ($12.36) and DCF midpoint ($14.22) diverge by 15% because the DCF capitalises post-2031 FCF at 2.25% growth, while the P/E target holds a 12x exit.

3. Bias check

Upside scenario weights (Thesis + Aggressive) = 30%; downside weights (Stress + Conservative) = 35%; Base = 35%. The distribution leans slightly to the downside. That is deliberate: the base deck already assumes gold falls ~9% to $3,800, real yields are rising, and the one verified quarter was a large miss. We did not rebalance.
