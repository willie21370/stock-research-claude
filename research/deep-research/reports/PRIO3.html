Section 1 — Business architecture, unit economics, moat & contractual visibility

Business model. PRIO is Brazil’s largest independent oil producer. It buys mature or under-invested offshore fields from majors and NOCs, then redevelops them: revitalisation, infill wells, clustering and tie-backs. The assets are the Valente cluster (Frade + Wahoo tie-back), the Bravo cluster (Polvo + Tubarão Martelo), Albacora Leste and Peregrino (operator; 100% after buying Equinor’s 60% and Sinochem’s 40% ASSUMED for deal terms). Moat type: cost advantage plus a process intangible (integration and redevelopment know-how). Competitive insulation: 3/5.

Moat durability: static to slightly eroding. Low lifting cost is partly structural (clustering) and partly a function of plateau volumes. Unit costs will rise as fields decline from ~2029. The force most likely to erode it is natural decline plus competition for mature-asset M&A (Brava, PetroReconcavo, international funds), which raises entry prices. We expect visible erosion by 2029–30 unless new acquisitions are made.

Build-vs-buy / copy risk. An incumbent (Petrobras, Shell, Equinor) can do this, but majors are sellers of these assets by strategy, so replication is unlikely. Hyperscaler or platform risk does not apply. Customers (refiners and traders) cannot "build in-house". As an acquisition target: plausible but improbable. A buyer would need to pay ~US$14–17bn EV (a 20–35% premium) for a Brazilian-only portfolio, with possible buyers being Middle-East NOCs or Asian NOCs seeking Brazilian barrels. This adds only a weak floor.

Customer ROI / loss avoidance. PRIO sells a fungible commodity. Its "value" to buyers is reliable medium-sour or heavy (Peregrino) crude at a Brent-linked discount. The 2Q26 improvement in commercial discount is a real but small edge. Pricing power in a downturn is nil. Customer-ROI defensibility: 2/10.
