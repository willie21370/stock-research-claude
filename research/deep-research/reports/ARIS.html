Section 1 — Business architecture, unit economics, moat & contractual visibility

Business model: Aris owns two producing underground gold operations in Antioquia/Caldas, Colombia. Segovia (2025: 227,762 oz) runs owner-operated mines plus about 40+ contract-mining partners (CMPs). The CMPs mine and sell ore to Aris at gold-linked prices. Marmato (H1-26: 17.1koz) has an upper vein mine now and a Bulk Mining Zone feeding a new 5,000 tpd CIP plant from Q4-26. Unmodelled options are Soto Norte (Santander, large development project awaiting licensing) and Toroparu (Guyana) ASSUMED (2026 status NOT RELIABLY AVAILABLE).

Moat classification: cost advantage through ore grade. The moat is grade 2/5. Gold is a commodity, so Aris is a price-taker with no pricing power. The defensible assets are the deposits themselves (Segovia's 10.23 g/t head grade in Q2-26) and the CMP social contract. Durability: roughly static. Grade cannot be copied, but it erodes if mining moves deeper and costs rise, or if illegal mining takes ground. The biggest eroding force is security deterioration around Segovia, which could hit at any time in the window. A slower one is Colombian fiscal policy (royalty and tax changes) over 2027–28.

Build-vs-buy / copy risk: no incumbent can copy an ore body. The relevant rivals are other Colombian producers (e.g., Zijin's Buriticá) and illegal miners. No platform or "hyperscaler" risk applies, and customers (refiners) cannot make gold in-house. M&A optionality: Aris is a credible target. At ~500koz/yr it would suit a mid-tier or major wanting Andean growth (e.g., Zijin, which already operates in Colombia, or a larger Canadian gold producer). A typical 30–40% premium applied to $17.50 gives ~$23–25 ASSUMED. This is a partial floor in non-stress worlds; Colombian risk limits the buyer pool.

Customer ROI / loss-avoidance: not applicable in the usual sense. Gold is sold at spot to refiners and banks, and there is no premium to defend. During budget freezes, demand for gold has historically held or risen (safe-haven flows). Customer-ROI defensibility: 3/10, purely a price-taker.
