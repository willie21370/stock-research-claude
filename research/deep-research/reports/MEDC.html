New capital should keep only a small pilot below about Rp1,250.

Executive summary

  1. Operations are executing at the top of guidance. H1-26 production was 170 mboepd (+18.9% y/y), the top of the 165–170 range, with Q2 peaking at 173. Cash cost of US$9.0/boe is below the <US$10 guide. Power sales rose 17% to 2,323 GWh. The operating engine is not the risk.
  2. 2026 earnings are a peak, not a run-rate. H1 net profit was US$275.3M, of which US$207.9M came in Q2. That reflects a war-driven oil spike (Brent peaked near US$110) and AMMN's smelter reaching full capacity. Our base 2026E is US$540M (EPS Rp383), but EPS falls 11% in 2027 and 6% in 2028 as Brent normalises to US$75 and then US$70. This is a falling earnings line.
  3. The valuation is cheap, but cheap for reasons. The stock trades at 4.1x 2027E P/E and ~3.2x EV/EBITDA. A reverse DCF shows the price implies ~US$58 Brent. The discount reflects Indonesian country risk (heavy September foreign outflows, IHSG ~6,277), a holdco/associate structure, family control and a volatile AMMN contribution. None of these looks likely to reverse quickly.
  4. Deleveraging is the quiet compounder. Base FCF of US$440–470M/yr less ~US$100M of dividends cuts net debt from ~US$2.4bn to ~US$1.1bn by end-2029 (0.8x EBITDA). That supports a ~4.5% dividend yield but does not move the 36-month price far enough for the mandate.
  5. The return distribution fails the mandate. Probabilities are 10/25/40/18/7 (stress → aggressive). The probability-weighted 36m value is Rp1,622 (+17.9%). Only the 7% Aggressive scenario clears 2x. The upside/downside ratio is 0.28:1, against a target of ≥3:1.

Section 1 — Business architecture, unit economics, moat & contractual visibility

Business model & moat

Medco is Indonesia's largest listed private upstream oil and gas producer. It has operated and non-operated PSCs in Indonesia (South Natuna Sea Block B, Corridor, Rimau/South Sumatra, Senoro and others), Oman, Thailand and other markets. It also owns Medco Power (gas-fired IPPs, the Ijen geothermal plant and East Bali solar, selling to PLN under long-term PPAs) and an equity-accounted stake in PT Amman Mineral Internasional (AMMN), which owns the Batu Hijau copper-gold mine and smelter. Production was 170 mboepd in H1-26 and about 72% gas.

Customer ROI / loss-avoidance

Medco's gas avoids LNG-import costs for PLN, PGN and Singapore buyers. Its power PPAs give PLN baseload or renewable capacity. The value is measurable in $/mmbtu against LNG parity, but Indonesia's regulated gas prices (the HGBT policy for industrial users) limit pricing power for domestic gas. In a budget freeze, PLN offtake under take-or-pay PPAs holds up. Spot oil has no pricing power. Customer-ROI defensibility: 5/10.

Base-rate check

The base case assumes flat to slightly declining revenue (−1.4% CAGR 2026–29) and flat production. That is not a top-decile assumption. Holding about 170 mboepd for five years does require roughly 100% reserve replacement, which mid-size E&Ps manage in perhaps 40–50% of cases organically. Medco has historically bought its replacement barrels (Ophir 2019, Corridor stake, Oman). The aggressive production path (190 mboepd) would require M&A.

Expected value cross-check

The probability-weighted value is Rp1,622, against a DCF midpoint of Rp2,162. They differ by 33%, which is more than the 25% tolerance. We trust the multiple-based weighted value. Reflexivity / second-order check

The valuation does not depend on cheap equity capital, because the company self-funds. It does depend on (i) open USD bond markets to roll the 2027–2030 notes, and (ii) foreign flows back into Indonesian equities for any multiple re-rating. These are financial-market risks that fail fast, around sovereign or rupiah events. Operating risk (decline rates, AMMN output) is separate and fails slowly over several quarters.
