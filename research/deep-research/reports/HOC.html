Section 1 — Business architecture, unit economics, moat & contractual visibility

Business model & moat classification

Hochschild is a London-listed, Lima-headquartered underground and open-pit precious-metals miner. It has three operating assets:

  • Inmaculada (Peru, 100%; gold-silver underground vein)
  • San José (Argentina, 51%; 49% McEwen Mining; silver-gold)
  • Mara Rosa (Brazil, 100%; open-pit gold; first gold 2024)

Its pipeline is Monte do Carmo (Brazil; decision H2 2026), Royropata (Peru) and Volcan (Chile) ASSUMED.

Moat classification: cost/asset-quality advantage only, and only partial. Gold and silver are fungible, so there are no network effects, switching costs or IP. Competitive insulation: 2/5.

Moat durability: eroding. AISC rose from $1,873 (H1-25) to $2,448/GEO (H1-26) VERIFIED. The eroding force is cost inflation linked to the metal price itself (royalties, Peru's 8% workers' profit share, stronger soles and reais, Argentine peso inflation) plus grade depletion at San José. This erosion is already visible in 2026 and continues through ~2028 until MDC adds lower-cost ounces.

Build-vs-buy / copy risk

  • Incumbent replication: the orebodies cannot be copied. But a major (Newmont, Barrick, Agnico, Zijin) could buy comparable LatAm ounces for ~$1–2bn and build in 3–5 years.
  • Hyperscaler / platform: not applicable.
  • Customer in-house build: not applicable. Refiners and traders buy doré/concentrate and have no reason to integrate upstream.
  • M&A optionality: credible but family-gated. Hochschild family control (~35% ASSUMED) means any bid needs the chairman's consent. Plausible buyers: Zijin / CMOC-type Chinese consolidators, Agnico, Pan American or Fresnillo (Peñoles family overlap in Mexico/Peru silver). Typical precious-metals premium is 25–40%. M&A is a soft floor, not a valuation pillar.

Customer ROI / loss-avoidance

The product prevents no customer pain. It is a monetary and industrial commodity (silver: PV and electronics; gold: central banks, investment, jewellery). Value is set by the LBMA/COMEX price, not by the customer. Pricing power in a budget freeze is zero, but demand is never lost: every ounce is saleable. Customer-ROI defensibility: 3/10.
