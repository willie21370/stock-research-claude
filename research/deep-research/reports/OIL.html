Why this advice: Oil India is running well. Q1 FY27 (Apr–Jun 2026) set records: standalone PAT was ₹2,870 cr (+253% y/y) and consolidated PAT ₹4,027 cr, with oil output +11% y/y. The Numaligarh refinery (NRL) tripling to 9 MTPA is 84% physically complete. The problem is where those numbers come from. Q1 rode a war-inflated crude realisation of US$98.7/bbl and an extraordinary NRL GRM of US$36/bbl. As Brent normalises toward US$70–73, our base EPS falls from ₹66 (FY27E) to ₹49 (FY28E), which is 23% below consensus. The newly commissioned refinery train adds revenue but little profit at first, because its interest and depreciation start immediately. Existing holders can keep a modest position for the ~3% dividend yield and trim above ₹530. New capital should wait for ₹380–410.

Section 1 — Business architecture, unit economics, moat & contractual visibility

Business model & moat classification

Oil India is India's second-largest national E&P. It operates onshore nomination fields in Upper Assam and Arunachal Pradesh, plus Rajasthan (heavy oil and gas), with exploration in the Andaman, Mahanadi and KG basins. It owns the Naharkatiya–Barauni crude pipeline and a 69.63% stake ASSUMED in Numaligarh Refinery (NRL), which is expanding from 3 to 9 MTPA. It also holds overseas equity in Russia (Vankor, Taas-Yuryakh), Mozambique Area 1 LNG (part of the Indian consortium's 30%) and others, and minority stakes in IOCL and BPCL. Moat type: efficient scale plus regulatory licences (nomination acreage and Northeast infrastructure). Competitive insulation: 3/5. No one can easily replicate the Assam acreage and pipeline network. But OIL has no pricing power, because crude is Brent-linked and gas prices are set by the government.

Moat durability: static to slowly eroding. Mature Assam fields need constant workover spending just to hold output. The force most likely to erode the moat is reservoir decline plus open-acreage (OALP/DSF) competition for new blocks, visible by FY29–30 if exploration success stalls. Over the very long term, the energy transition in India's transport demand matters after 2035, outside our window.

Build-vs-buy / copy risk

  • Could a well-capitalised incumbent replicate it? Not quickly. Nomination blocks are allocated, not auctioned, and Northeast logistics take a decade to build. ONGC operates nearby, but in separate acreage.
  • Hyperscaler or platform risk: not applicable.
  • Customer in-sourcing: refiners (IOCL, BPCL) cannot produce crude. NRL is OIL's own downstream arm.
  • M&A optionality: an ONGC–OIL merger has been floated in policy circles before ASSUMED. Any such deal would be government-directed and could come at no premium to minorities. We give it no value as a floor.

Customer ROI / loss-avoidance

OIL sells a commodity. Its value to customers is secure domestic supply (crude to NRL and the IOCL Assam refineries, gas to BCPL, power and fertiliser plants), which avoids import freight and FX costs. Prices are benchmark-linked, not value-based. In a "budget freeze" (demand slump) pricing simply follows Brent. Customer-ROI defensibility: 3/10.

Revenue quality: 5/10.

Customer concentration & visibility

Top customers are PSU refiners (NRL, IOCL Guwahati/Digboi/Bongaigaon) and Northeast gas offtakers (BCPL, APGCL, NEEPCO, BVFCL). Top-customer share is NOT RELIABLY AVAILABLE; we assume more than 50% of crude goes to NRL and the IOCL Assam refineries ASSUMED. Gas offtake is a real constraint: Q1 FY27 gas output fell 8% y/y because BCPL and power plants took less. That is why the 5 BCM FY29 target depends on pipelines (the Indradhanush Northeast gas grid).
