Section 1 — Business architecture, unit economics, moat & contractual visibility

Business model & moat

Archer has four segments: Platform Operations (drilling and maintenance crews on operator-owned fixed platforms in the Norwegian and UK North Sea), Well Services (P&A, wireline, Oiltools; this now includes Premium Oilfield Services, a USD 20M fishing-tool acquisition closed 2 Oct 2025), Land Drilling (Argentina, mainly Vaca Muerta; the southern workover business was sold in Q1-26) and a small Renewables/geothermal unit (e.g. Nevis). The model is asset-light in platform drilling and asset-heavier in land rigs and tool rental. Moat type: switching costs and incumbency on platform contracts, plus a niche P&A technology edge. Competitive insulation: 2.5/5. Contracts are re-tendered; competitors such as Odfjell Technology and KCA Deutag can bid credibly, but changing a platform crew is costly and risky for the operator.

Moat durability: Static to slightly widening in P&A through 2029 because decommissioning liabilities build. It is eroding over the long run as Norwegian fixed-platform drilling declines with basin maturity and platform electrification. The force most likely to erode it is operator insourcing and consolidation of drilling contracts after 2030, together with field shutdowns.

Build-vs-buy / copy risk

  • Incumbent replication: Odfjell Technology, KCA Deutag, Saipem and SLB could bid for any Archer contract. The barrier is crew certification and operating track record, not capital. A competitor could take a contract at the next tender within 12–24 months.
  • Hyperscaler/platform owner: Not applicable.
  • Customer in-house: Equinor and Aker BP have historically outsourced platform drilling. Insourcing is possible but adds headcount and liability, so we see low probability (<10% over the window).
  • M&A target? Credible. Logical buyers are Odfjell Technology (consolidation), KCA Deutag, Weatherford, Expro or a private-equity sponsor. A 30–40% premium is plausible; at current EV/EBITDA of ~4x an acquirer could pay 5–5.5x and still be accretive. A sale would require refinancing the bond (change-of-control put assumed). We give it ~7% probability (Aggressive scenario).

Customer ROI / loss-avoidance

Archer prevents lost production, because platform drilling keeps producing fields productive, and it lets operators retire statutory P&A liabilities cheaply and safely. Rigless and light-well-intervention P&A is materially cheaper than a semi-submersible campaign, which is a measurable saving (day-rate × days) that operators track. Pricing power in a budget freeze is weak in drilling because operators defer infill wells. It is resilient in P&A, where the obligations are regulatory. Customer-ROI defensibility: 6/10.

Unit economics (Hardware/industrial block, adapted for oilfield services)

Key metrics are EBITDA margin per contract and crew utilisation. Group EBITDA margin moved from 12.5% (Q1-25) to 13.4% (Q1-26) to 17.1% (Q2-26). Contracts carry cost-indexation clauses (wage and CPI), which passes Norwegian wage inflation through with a lag. That mechanism is the inflation-beneficiary angle from the screen.
